---# ContentDesk AEO Positioning Spec

## Two-Sentence Explanation

ContentDesk is an AI-search visibility operator for lean founders: it finds the gaps stopping their product from showing up in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Google AI answers, then helps ship the next best asset or action to close those gaps.

It is interesting because AEO tools are becoming good at measurement and recommendations, but small teams still need the execution layer: a focused system that keeps improving their odds of being found, understood, trusted, and cited without hiring an SEO agency.

## Simple English Version

ContentDesk helps founders answer one recurring question:

```text
What should we ship this week to improve our chances of showing up when buyers ask AI tools what product to use?
```

The product does not promise guaranteed rankings, traffic, or AI citations. It promises a trustworthy operating loop:

```text
Track the right prompts
-> find where the brand is missing
-> diagnose why competitors or other sources are showing up
-> create the most useful asset or action
-> get founder approval
-> publish or hand off
-> recheck visibility later
```

## Mission

Help lean founders become easier for AI answer engines and search engines to find, understand, trust, and cite.

ContentDesk should not be a generic blog generator. It should be a specialist workflow for AI-search visibility, starting with content because content is the most controllable first asset.

## The Market Problem

Search is changing from a list of links into generated answers. Buyers increasingly ask tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Google AI:

```text
What tool should I use for this problem?
What are the best apps for this workflow?
Which product is better for my use case?
What alternatives should I consider?
```

Founders want to show up in those answers, but the market is still early and confusing.

Current options often fall into three buckets:

- Dashboards that show visibility, prompt mentions, competitors, and citations.
- Agencies that claim to know AEO or SEO best practices, often with unclear proof.
- Generic marketing agents that try to cover every growth channel at once.

For a solo founder, all three can feel incomplete:

```text
Dashboards create homework.
Agencies are expensive and hard to trust.
Horizontal agents can feel unfocused.
```

The unresolved job is not simply monitoring. The unresolved job is:

```text
Keep doing the right SEO/AEO work every week without making the founder become a specialist.
```

## The Trust Constraint

ContentDesk should avoid fake certainty.

Do not promise:

```text
We will get you cited everywhere.
We know the algorithm.
We can attribute every AI mention to revenue.
```

Promise instead:

```text
We keep improving your odds of being recommended by AI search without hiring an SEO agency.
```

ContentDesk earns trust by showing:

- which prompts were tracked
- where the brand was missing
- which competitors appeared
- which sources were cited
- what gap was identified
- what asset or action was shipped
- whether visibility changed after a reasonable recheck window

## SEO, AEO, GEO, and LLMO

AEO, GEO, and LLMO are different names for the same broad shift:

```text
Make the brand visible and citeable in AI-generated answers.
```

ContentDesk should start from AEO because it is the sharper wedge:

```text
AI answer visibility for lean founders.
```

But the work overlaps with SEO. Search engines, AI answer engines, and LLMs all need clear, useful, structured, trustworthy content and corroborating sources.

The practical framing:

```text
SEO helps people find you through search results.
AEO helps AI answer engines recommend or cite you in generated answers.
ContentDesk starts with AEO-informed content, but the assets should also strengthen SEO.
```

## Prompt Coverage

Prompt coverage is the AEO strategy layer.

Instead of asking only:

```text
What keywords do we rank for?
```

ContentDesk asks:

```text
What buyer questions should our brand be the answer to?
```

Examples:

```text
Best AI model photo apps for Shopify
How do I turn flat-lays into model photos?
What are the best Botika alternatives?
Which Shopify apps create AI product videos?
How can small fashion brands create model photos without a photoshoot?
```

Each prompt should map to:

- buyer journey phase
- customer intent
- current answer
- competitors mentioned
- sources cited
- missing proof or asset
- recommended next action

The core prompt workflow:

```text
Prompt
-> current AI answer
-> brand mentioned or missing
-> competitors mentioned
-> cited sources
-> gap diagnosis
-> asset/action to ship
```

## Buyer Journey Phases

ContentDesk should organize prompts and assets around the buyer journey:

```text
Awareness -> Consideration -> Evaluation -> Decision
```

- Awareness: buyers are naming the problem, pain, workflow, or category.
- Consideration: buyers are looking for tools, apps, workflows, or solution types.
- Evaluation: buyers are comparing vendors, alternatives, features, proof, and tradeoffs.
- Decision: buyers are checking pricing, implementation, fit, trust, reviews, and next steps.

This keeps ContentDesk from generating random content. Every asset should exist because it helps answer a real buyer question at a specific phase.

## What ContentDesk Outputs Over Time

Long term, ContentDesk can output many types of AEO actions:

- blog posts
- product pages
- category pages
- comparison pages
- alternatives pages
- FAQ sections
- structured data recommendations
- Shopify App Store listing updates
- YouTube demo scripts
- Reddit/community reply drafts
- third-party outreach packets
- partner/listing page pitches
- content refreshes
- recheck reports

It can also eventually branch into technical SEO/AEO hygiene recommendations:

- `/llms.txt` formatting and site maps for AI-readable context
- title and meta description fixes
- canonical link fixes
- default and per-page Open Graph image fixes
- schema / structured data recommendations
- crawlability checks for content hidden behind client-side JavaScript
- internal linking improvements
- implementation-ready Codex handoff tasks for repo-backed sites

But the first version should start smaller.

## MVP Focus

Start with one narrow, painful workflow:

```text
AEO-informed blog and page briefs for Shopify app founders.
```

The MVP should help a founder go from:

```text
I need search and AI visibility, but I do not know what to write.
```

to:

```text
Here is the next founder-approved content kit to publish, and here is why it matters.
```

For now, ContentDesk should focus on blogs and content kits because they are:

- controllable by the founder
- easy to approve
- useful for both SEO and AEO
- a natural foundation before outreach, video, or community workflows

Technical hygiene should stay post-MVP. The broader loop is the same, but the artifact changes:

```text
recommendation -> validation -> implementation-ready fix/kit -> Codex handoff -> recheck
```

For content recommendations, the output is a publish-ready content kit. For technical hygiene recommendations, the output should be a repo-aware implementation packet that Codex can apply safely.

## The Initial Product Loop

```text
Brand Profile
-> prompt/topic opportunity
-> research and source grounding
-> founder chooses topic
-> article brief
-> draft
-> QA for usefulness, specificity, and citeability
-> publish kit
-> manual handoff
```

This is intentionally not a full marketing team. It is a repeatable weekly content operating loop.

## What Makes This 10x

ContentDesk should be 10x by being more focused and more executable than the alternatives.

Not:

```text
Here are 40 SEO recommendations.
Here is a dashboard.
Here is a generic AI blog post.
Here is a full-stack marketing agent for everything.
```

Instead:

```text
Here is the highest-leverage content gap for your product right now.
Here is the buyer prompt it maps to.
Here is why competitors or other sources are winning.
Here is the approved publish-ready asset.
Here is when we will recheck it.
```

The product should reduce founder decision-making, not increase it.

## Narrow Wedge

The strongest starting wedge:

```text
ContentDesk helps Shopify app founders ship weekly SEO/AEO content that improves their odds of being recommended by AI search.
```

This is narrower than:

```text
AI marketing agent for every channel.
```

But it is broader and more valuable than:

```text
AI blog writer.
```

The principle:

```text
Broad execution later, narrow promise now.
```

## Product North Star

ContentDesk should become the system a founder trusts to keep AI-search visibility work moving.

The founder should feel:

```text
I do not need to become an SEO or AEO expert.
ContentDesk knows my product, watches the gaps, and brings me the next useful thing to approve.
```

## Positioning Line

```text
ContentDesk is the AI-search visibility operator for lean founders.
```

Expanded:

```text
ContentDesk finds the gaps stopping your product from showing up in AI answers, turns those gaps into founder-approved content, and tracks whether your visibility improves over time.
```
